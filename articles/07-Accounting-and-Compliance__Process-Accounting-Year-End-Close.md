How Do I Process Accounting Year-End Close?

Category: Accounting and Compliance Time: 4-8 hours (spread over several days) Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Expert

March 2026 terminology: Use Contributions Center (plural), not "Contribution Center." Cash receipts path: Accounting Center → Accounting → Cash Receipts. Adjustments: Accounting Center → Accounting → Adjustments. Navigation to PERAC reporting and late-step Period Close / Accounting Periods entries in this document should be verified in the current shell.

What This Does

The Accounting Year-End Close is a comprehensive process that finalizes all financial activity for the fiscal year, prepares the system for a new fiscal year, and ensures accurate financial reporting and regulatory compliance. This critical workflow closes out the old year, reconciles all accounts, generates required reports, and sets up the foundation for the upcoming year's operations.

Why This is Important:

Financial Integrity: Ensures accurate year-end financial statements
Regulatory Compliance: Required for PERAC, IRS, and audit requirements
System Readiness: Prepares system for new fiscal year
Audit Support: Creates documentation for annual audit
Historical Record: Preserves permanent financial snapshot
Member Services: Enables accurate benefit calculations

Major Components:

Final transaction posting and reconciliation
Year-end adjustments and accruals
Financial statement generation
Calculation population (interest and service)
10-year records update
Regulatory reporting (1099-R, PERAC, etc.)
System preparation for new year
Audit documentation and archival

Step-by-Step Instructions
Step 1: Pre-Close Verification (Week 1)

Purpose: Ensure all fiscal year transactions are recorded before beginning close process.

Verification Checklist:

✅ Payroll Complete:

1. Navigate to Payment Center (or Contributions Center for payroll history)
2. Review payroll history for fiscal year
3. Verify:
   - All pay periods processed
   - No pending payrolls
   - Final annual payroll reconciled
   - Employer contributions posted
4. Run payroll summary report
5. Compare to budget/expectations


✅ Contributions Recorded:

1. Navigate to Contribution Center
2. Review contribution batches
3. Verify:
   - All employers submitted
   - All periods covered
   - Totals reconcile
4. Run contribution summary report
5. Document any late contributions


✅ Benefit Payments Complete:

1. Navigate to Payment Center
2. Review payment history
3. Verify:
   - Final monthly payments processed
   - All disbursements recorded
   - No pending payments
4. Reconcile payment totals


✅ Cash Receipts Recorded:

1. Navigate to Accounting > Cash Receipts
2. Review all receipts for final month
3. Verify deposits match bank statement
4. Document any receipts in transit


✅ Investment Transactions:

1. Review investment activity
2. Verify all trades recorded
3. Confirm income postings
4. Match to investment custodian statements


Document Findings:

List any missing items
Assign responsibility
Set deadlines for completion
Track resolution

Common Questions

Q: How long does year-end close take? A: Typically 8-12 weeks from fiscal year-end to completion. Factors: organization size, complexity, staff experience, system capabilities. First time may take longer. Plan for 12-16 weeks to be safe.

Q: When should we start year-end planning? A: Begin planning 2-3 months before fiscal year-end. Review procedures, update checklists, confirm resource availability, schedule key events, communicate timeline.

Q: Can we close the year before the audit? A: Best practice: Close after audit fieldwork complete. Some systems do preliminary close, then reopen for audit adjustments. Never do final close until all audit issues resolved.

Q: What if we miss a transaction? A: Depends on timing and materiality. Before statements issued: Correct and revise. After statements issued: May require prior period adjustment. Consult with auditors and management.

Q: How do we handle the new GASB pronouncements? A: Review new requirements during planning. Assess impact on your system. Implement required changes. Update accounting policies. Disclose in notes. Consult with auditors early.

Q: Should we close monthly periods during year-end? A: Some systems "soft close" prior months but keep year open. Others hard close each month. Choose approach that balances control with flexibility. Document policy clearly.

Q: What if calculation population has errors? A: Don't proceed with close until errors resolved. Investigate causes. Make corrections. Rerun population. Verify results. Document resolution. Large errors may require management notification.

Q: How do we coordinate with the audit? A: Early communication is key. Share timeline. Provide PBC list responses timely. Assign staff to support fieldwork. Schedule entrance and exit conferences. Document all requests and responses.

Q: What's the single most important thing? A: Reconciliation. Everything must reconcile - banks, investments, control accounts, subsidiary ledgers. Never force a reconciliation. Find and fix differences. This prevents most year-end problems.
